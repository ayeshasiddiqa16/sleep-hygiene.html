The Biology of Sleep

Meet the night crew that runs your body 🌙

Sleep is regulated by your body's circadian clock and several biological processes and hormones play a key role. Light, the circadian clock and hormone balance all work together to control when you sleep and when you wake. Here's the crew on duty every night:

🌙

Melatonin

Signals that it's biological night and helps you fall asleep. It rises in the evening as it gets dark.

The sleepy time hormone
☀️

Cortisol

Helps with alertness and the sleep wake cycle. It's usually higher in the morning to get you going.

The wake-up call
🧠

Brain

Controls sleep and alertness #sorted memories, flushing out waste and recharging you as you rest.

Mission control
🫀

Heart & Body

Regulates your physical health overnight repair, muscle growth and a steady, healthy heartbeat.

Night maintenance
☀️ 🌙
🛌

Your circadian rhythm : a built-in 24-hour cycle that tells your body when to feel awake and when to wind down. 🕐

Sleep Deprivation vs Insomnia

Two different night-time villains 🦹

They sound similar, but they're not the same thing. One is about not enough time asleep the other is about not enough quality sleep even when there is time.

🔋

Sleep Deprivation

Not getting enough sleep, often because there simply isn't enough time for it. Late night study sessions, early alarms, scrolling "just one more video"… the battery never fully recharges.

VS
🛏️

Insomnia

Persistent difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or getting good quality sleep despite having enough time for it. You're in bed, lights off… but your brain didn't get the memo.

💡 They are different problems, although they can overlap.

The Effects of Poor Sleep

What happens when the night shift goes off duty 😵‍💫

Missing sleep isn't just about feeling sleepy. It ripples through your brain, your emotions and your body — starting from the very first night.

🧠

Brain

  • Reduced concentration
  • Slower reaction times
  • Difficulty learning
  • Poor multi-tasking
😟

Emotions

  • Irritability
  • Mood swings
  • Feeling overwhelmed
  • More stress
🫀

Body

  • Tension headaches
  • Fatigue
  • Weaker immune system
  • Higher risk of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure & high cholesterol (long-term)

Immediate Effects · 1–2 nights

  • Less focus
  • Lower performance
  • Mood changes

Long-Term Risks · chronic sleep deprivation

  • Type 2 diabetes
  • High blood pressure
  • High cholesterol
